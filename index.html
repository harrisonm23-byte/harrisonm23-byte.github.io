About

I work on model evaluations at SpaceXAI. In my own research, I focus on technical AI safety and AI governance.

My technical research focuses on how internal states can predict misaligned behavior. In my safety evaluations of long-horizon agents, I study agent behavior through the lens of behavioral economics. I ask how valence framing, responsibility attribution, and social conformity can shift an agent’s internal states and subsequent actions over many steps. I’m also interested in white-box methods that use internal states to predict and steer behavior. My latest paper, A Push Down the Mountain, tests these questions on collusion in a market simulation.

My governance research studies how evaluations can inform regulatory design, and how we can analogize AI risks to existing industrial hazards for more efficient regulatory responses. In The Ratio Method, I break down AI risks into consistent law-and-economics variables, such as frequency, average damages, causation mechanism, and litigation costs. I then find industrial harms that are similar across these variables, and draw from the regimes that govern them.

Research interests: agent safety evaluations, interpretability and steering, the macroeconomics of AI and technical AI governance.

Research

AI Safety and Evaluations

  • A Push Down the Mountain

    Framing Effects and Internal State Interventions on Collusive Behavior in a Long-Horizon LLM Agent

    Working paper, October 2026 (submitted to arXiv) · PDF · Code

    Gemma 3 12B is asked to collude across 260 episodes of a market built from the London silver-fixing case. Blame framing raised participation from 5% to 33%. A second phase tests whether internal states can monitor and steer the behavior.

AI Governance and Liability

  • Precedents

    Matching Frontier AI Risks to Liability Regimes

    Report, 2026 · PDF · Code

    Profiles the eight frontier AI risks of the International AI Safety Report 2026 on law-and-economics variables, and matches each to the closest of sixteen liability regimes.

  • An Analytical Comparison

    of the Ratio Method and Amodei’s Policy on the AI Exponential

    Essay, June 2026 · PDF

    Traces where Dario Amodei’s 2026 governance proposals converge with the 2021 Ratio Method, and separates preventive regulation from liability after harm.

  • The Ratio Method

    Addressing Complex Tort Liability in the Fourth Industrial Revolution (with Grant Frazier)

    St. Mary’s Law Journal, vol. 52, p. 679, 2021 · PDF

    A law-and-economics framework for designing liability rules for AI cybersecurity, connected devices, gene editing, and catastrophic risks such as biological weapons. It profiles each risk on seven variables, including frequency and average damages, and matches it to precedent regimes.
